\section{Independent Batched Evolution Experiment Protocol}
\label{app:concrete-protocol}

\paragraph{Protocol and evidence status.}
The stratified v6 independent batch amendment is specified in
\path{experiments/independent_protocol_v6_stratified.json}; its exact task membership
and source orders are recorded in \path{experiments/joint_task_splits_v6_stratified.json},
with the human-readable index \path{experiments/task_assignments_v6_stratified.md}.
This is an experimental
protocol, not a report of completed model experiments. Metadata generation
and software checks do not establish end-to-end support for new benchmarks
in the older v3 execution driver, nor supply empirical measurements.
The v1--v5 registrations and the already-started original v6 registration
remain immutable historical provenance. This amendment uses a separate
registration and starts each trajectory from zero; it does not reinterpret
or continue their runs, learned states or results.

\paragraph{Benchmark roles and exact membership.}
ResearchRubrics (RR), DeepSearchQA (DSQA), and WritingBench (WB) independently
supply evolution tasks and fixed validation tasks. DeepResearch Bench II,
IFEval, and single-turn IFBench supply external test tasks only. No separate
development split is reserved in v6. Unselected tasks are unused reserves,
not development data or automatically scheduled additional tests.

Frozen v4 parent boundaries and exposure exclusions constrain the amendment.
DSQA and WB resample 40 evolution, 10 validation and 50 test tasks within
their respective eligible parent partitions. RR retains all 30 parent
evolution tasks, reselects 10 of its 20 parent validation tasks and transfers
the remaining 10 to evolution. Its 33 historically clean-designated test
tasks remain fixed, including members with subsequently registered exposure.
The historical label does not assert that all retained tasks are currently
unexposed or suitable for a new blind confirmation test.
Deterministic public-domain quotas and fixed hash ranks, never scores,
define membership before new execution. RR retains all 33 historically clean-designated
test tasks; its 18 historically exposed/reserved tasks are not selected
in this track and retain their exposure flags in the v4 parent inventory.
Historical pilot or gated-update scores are not new v6 measurements.

WB uses the pinned 1,000-task release, not the earlier 1,239-task release.
Its subset balances public top-level domain quotas, then language within
each domain, with deterministic ranks.
Official WB index 1 was exposed during inventory preparation and remains
eligible only for evolution or unused reserve, never validation or test;
the stratified selection preserves that restriction. IFEval samples 50 of its 541
evaluation prompts regardless of the hosting service's split label;
IFBench samples 50 of the pinned 300-task single-turn release, not its
training constraints or multi-turn variant. DR Bench II samples 40 of 132
tasks with balanced public-theme quotas and 20 English and 20 Chinese;
translated-pair structure is not assumed. The five transfer-test tasks
registered as exposed during development on 2026-10-04, and any additional
known transfer-benchmark exposures in that register, are excluded before resampling.
The extent of incidental exposure in some historical runs is unknown;
retained historical test members are not claimed to be a new blind test.
IFEval and IFBench do not provide trusted domain fields, so
their fixed-hash samples do not imply domain balance.
The unused 2,551 tasks cannot be added after inspecting outcomes
without a new outcome-independent registration and exposure audit.

Evolution (training), validation and test subsets each aim for equal domain
quotas and similar domain proportions, subject to available inventory,
intact duplicate-question groups and fixed historical boundaries. RR uses
the release's public \texttt{domain}; DSQA uses \texttt{problem\_category}.
RR's parent validation pool covers only nine of its ten domains and lacks
Hypotheticals \& Philosophy, so its ten-task validation subset cannot
cover that domain. Its retained evolution members and fixed test set also
limit exact balance. The manifest reports observed domain/language counts,
capacity-constrained quota deviations, deviations from the equal ideal
$N/K$ over all release domains, and unavailable strata. Zero deviation from
capacity-constrained quotas does not establish uniformity of fixed RR
subsets or imply that a rare domain appears in every subset.

Native IDs, release-relative row references and question hashes in the
versioned manifest define ``which questions,'' not arbitrary ranges or
fresh shuffles on each execution. Only public metadata may determine new
memberships. Private criteria, answers and observed performance never do.
Exact duplicates and known source/semantic overlap must be audited across
all six datasets; conflicts require a versioned, outcome-independent
amendment. Question disjointness alone does not prove topic or pretraining
disjointness. Private reference data are not redistributed in public task
indexes.

\paragraph{Nine independent source trajectories.}
Each source/run starts from an empty rubric/organization/execution experience
store and the same fixed JIT generator archive. Source benchmarks do not
share evolved state. Each of the three sources has three runs and 40
evolution tasks, giving nine trajectories and eight five-task batches per
trajectory.
The three source-order seeds are 20261001, 20261002 and 20261003; they do not
change memberships or constitute guaranteed provider sampling seeds.
Every source task appears once per source/run, giving 40 source slots per
trajectory. The five tasks in one batch all read the same unchanged
experience and Agent Pool state; no attribution, persistent update or
evolution occurs between these tasks. After all five terminate, global
RubricGraph attribution and local role reflection inform three candidate
states, each branching from that same batch input state. The meta-agent
integrates the batch evidence and may evolve its design experience and full
agent harness prototypes. Pool Add, Delete/Prune, Split, Merge, Specialize
and Reorganize operations occur only during this evolution step. Construction
can create and record a temporary harness without mutating the Pool.

Batch boundaries occur at positions $5,10,15,20,25,30,35,40$. Positions
count attempted source slots, not successful updates or database versions.
Failed execution, incomplete evaluation or no valid proposal still consume
their registered slot; no replacement task or quality-triggered retry is
introduced. Interrupted requests require journal recovery rather than
fresh sampling. Structural and provenance checks precede candidate
publication. The next batch waits for its three candidates' validation and
starts from the selected whole-state winner. There is no extra source pass.

\paragraph{Fixed source validation after every batch.}
Each of the three candidates generates one artifact for every one of its
source's fixed ten validation tasks, giving 30 evaluation slots per batch
and $8\times3\times10=240$ per trajectory. The initial state is not a
fourth candidate and receives no validation slots. Validation is neither the most recent source batch
nor an expanding window. Snapshot stores are read-only, attribution is
disabled, and validation feedback cannot become experience or enter source
actors. Identical full-state and execution identities may reuse cached
outcomes with explicit provenance, not gain extra sampling opportunities.

Eligibility requires at least 9/10 complete evaluations on the candidate's
own source validation set. For task $t$, fixed
theoretical bounds $L_t,H_t$ define selection-only normalization
\begin{equation}
N_t(Q)=\begin{cases}
(Q-L_t)/(H_t-L_t), & H_t>L_t,\\
0, & H_t=L_t.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
RR uses its pinned signed-weight minimum and maximum: for positive weight
sum $W_t^+$, $L_t=\sum_{j:w_j<0}w_j/W_t^+$ and $H_t=1$; a zero denominator
has the pinned degenerate interval $[0,0]$. DSQA uses $[0,1]$, preserving
its native F1. WB uses $[1,10]$, hence $(Q-1)/9$. Bounds are fixed before
observing outcomes, not fitted by checkpoint or benchmark test performance.
Out-of-range purported scores are invalid, not silently repaired.

Let $\widetilde N= N_t(Q)$ for a complete evaluation and zero otherwise.
For source $b$, the selection objective for one batch candidate is
\begin{equation}
U_b(C)=\frac{1}{|V_b|}\sum_{t\in V_b}\widetilde N_{C,t}.
\end{equation}
No cross-source validation aggregation selects a candidate. Missing native scores remain
null; the zero is solely a conservative
selection surrogate, not an invented official measurement. Completion
and feasible score intervals are reported separately.

After each batch, select among its three eligible candidates. Form the
maximum-relative tie set with tolerance $10^{-12}$, then prefer more complete
evaluations, earlier candidate index and lexical state hash. The winner
feeds the next batch; the eighth batch winner is the final selected state.
No eligible candidate makes the trajectory inconclusive rather than
replaceable by a favorable extra run. Validation scores select a state but
do not enter agent reflection or persistent experience.

\paragraph{One reusable generator version, not new weights.}
For a given source/run, the identical selected state hash is used on its
own source test subset and each of the three transfer benchmarks. JIT still
generates a task-conditioned MAS from the new public task and applicable
source experience; the selected object is not
a fixed Analyst/Evidence/Writer team for every possible task. Task-level
execution remains single-pass shared-ledger coordination, without iterative
inter-agent negotiation.

The released reusable meta-agent package binds model serving identity,
generator code and prompts, configuration, tool/retrieval policy, the
selected \texttt{ExperienceSnapshot}, and data/code/protocol hashes.
The snapshot contains reusable experience, not newly trained model
weights. All nine source trajectories are evaluated and reported. Each
source's default package is predefined as its run 0 eighth-batch winner;
test results do not choose a deployment source or run. The main experiment
uses independently evolved per-source states and reports their transfer
performance separately.

\paragraph{Frozen tests, controls and scoring.}
For each source, its three selected states generate one artifact per task
on that source's test set and all three transfer test sets. Each static
control generates one artifact per task across all six subsets in
total, shared across run comparisons rather than relabeled as three
independent observations. The five core methods are initial and selected
\method, direct one-call generation, matched-backbone native JIT and a
rubric-guided fixed single-pass MAS. Identical inputs, evidence, backbone
and resource ceilings apply across comparable conditions. A matched JIT
proxy backbone is not claimed to be the original JIT-27B checkpoint.
Terminal-state, G/GO and mechanism ablations are excluded from the mandatory
subset inventory.
They require a separate frozen registration before execution and are not
automatically enabled.

Register all task/state/repetition slots and selected source hashes first;
seal all submissions across all conditions and benchmarks before invoking
test judges or releasing test feedback. Test stores remain read-only with
attribution disabled. Exact-state artifact reuse requires explicit identity
and provenance, with actual and logical cost distinguished. Test failure
cannot trigger state changes, task replacement or a second selection.

Report native metrics separately: signed-weight rubric compliance on RR,
answer-set F1 on DSQA, native writing quality on WB, satisfied-criterion
fraction with a separate blocked-source rate on DR Bench II, and author
programmatic instruction-following metrics on IFEval/IFBench. Preserve
the exact pinned metric variant in the execution registration. A shared
LLM judge is used only where the task's scoring requires one; executable
instruction checkers do not become a proxy LLM rubric. Adapted judging
prompts and model substitutions must be disclosed, not represented as
official leaderboard reproductions. Do not average the six native score
scales into a purported overall leaderboard score.

For each source--benchmark effect, average that source's three selected trajectories and
compare with the single shared static-control artifact on each task, then
use paired whole-task uncertainty, not rubric/call-level pseudoreplication.
Report each trajectory separately too. A single artifact per state/task
does not estimate within-state output variance; the three seeds vary the
evolution order, not a controlled provider sampling seed. Small subsets
can yield wide intervals and do not establish full-dataset or SOTA results.
Missing evaluation remains null with completion,
failure counts and feasible bounds rather than complete-case superiority.
The selected-versus-initial contrasts form separate Holm families: six
primary research/writing contrasts (three source self-tests and three
source-to-DR-Bench-II transfers) and six instruction diagnostic contrasts
(three sources by IFEval/IFBench). The selected-versus-matched-JIT/rubric-fixed
contrasts likewise form separate families of twelve primary and twelve
diagnostic comparisons. No mechanism-test family is included in this
mandatory subset track; additional contrasts require their own registration.

\paragraph{Resources and execution boundary.}
Nine trajectories entail $9\times40=360$ evolution slots and
$9\times8\times3\times10=2,160$ validation slots. Their
$9\times8\times3=216$ candidate-generation jobs are accounted separately
from task-artifact slots. Selected-state testing requires
$3\times(33+50+50)+9\times(40+50+50)=1,659$ artifact slots. The four
shared static controls require $4\times273=1,092$, giving 2,751 test slots
and 5,271 nominal task slots before identical-state reuse. These are not API requests, token ceilings, guaranteed
successes or executed results. Evidence preparation, scoring, human audits
and optional separately registered experiments require their own accounting.
The user has authorized the amended campaign; this registration itself
records planned work rather than completed results.

Independent trajectories, five-task frozen batches, three candidate-generation
jobs and their validation tasks may run concurrently where dependencies
permit. A short request pressure test completed 80/80 requests with 64 task
workers, generator capacity 64 and judge capacity 16 (global fallback 80).
This checks transport at those capacities, not sustained complete-task
performance. Next-batch release still waits for its own validation winner,
and test scoring still waits for the global submission seal.

The MAS generator is budget-aware at design time: its frozen policy receives
the task token ceiling, per-response ceiling, and live consumed/reserved/remaining
token ledger. Its structured team budget plan estimates input/output tokens,
model and tool calls, communication volume and later-stage reserves, with
quality--cost trade-offs and feasibility checked against the remaining budget.
Expected calls do not impose a fixed number of collaboration rounds. Measure
actual tokens and failures alongside native quality, distinguish estimates from
observations, and leave unknown prices null. Cost experience may be updated
only on evolution tasks; validation and testing remain read-only. The registered
validation quality and tie-breaking rules are unchanged: no retrospective token
penalty selects a different checkpoint. Additional budget levels or a control
without budget awareness require separate registration.

Freeze per-task public evidence, serving metadata, prompts, code, resource
ceilings and any retry policy before running the amended execution
inventory. Private criteria and target answers remain evaluator-only.
Previously implemented v3 evidence/resource boundaries are provenance,
not evidence that the v6 campaign has completed. Generated
Python local execution still requires explicit unsafe-local opt-in and is
not an OS sandbox. No SOTA or performance claim follows from the present
protocol, manifest checks, synthetic smoke tests or historical API probes.
