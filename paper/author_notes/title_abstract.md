# JIT-Compose: Evolving Multi-Agent Workflow Synthesis for Open-Ended Generation

## Abstract

Open-ended generation requires deciding not only what to write, but how agents should collaborate to produce it. When quality requirements are only partially specified, the central challenge is to anticipate what good work demands and turn those expectations into a suitable writing process. We introduce JIT-Compose, an experience-driven framework that synthesizes an executable, task-specific multi-agent writing workflow on demand. Predicted rubrics serve both as a specification for workflow construction and as a reference for improving future construction. Before execution, global–local planning refines the requirements and their importance, negotiates agent responsibilities and information dependencies, and instantiates the resulting design as roles, interactions, and verification stages. After submission, independent criterion-level feedback is aligned with the frozen predictions and examined against global coordination records and agent-local trajectories. This supports targeted revisions to requirement prediction, team organization, or execution, rather than an undifferentiated instruction to improve the answer. After attribution, scoped, structurally valid revisions enter versioned reusable experience directly, without an accept, hold, or reject quality-selection stage. Model parameters and the evaluator remain fixed, while committed experience changes how subsequent teams and workflows are generated. JIT-Compose thus couples just-in-time task adaptation with cross-task evolution: it retains knowledge for constructing the next writing process, not a single process that every task must reuse.

This is a method-and-narrative revision. No new empirical finding is asserted.
